Q1) BASIC COMPONENTS OF IC ENGINE AND THEIR FUNCTIONS

 1.  Cylinder Block
     * The main body of the engine.
     * Houses the cylinder(s), coolant passages, and provides mounting for other
       components.
     * Acts as the backbone of the engine structure.
 2.  Cylinder
     * A hollow cylindrical space inside the block where the piston moves up and
       down.
     * Provides the combustion chamber volume for burning the air-fuel mixture.
 3.  Piston
     * A cylindrical component that moves reciprocally inside the cylinder.
     * Converts pressure generated by combustion into linear motion.
 4.  Combustion Chamber
     * Space above the piston when it is at Top Dead Center (TDC).
     * Location where air-fuel mixture burns, producing high-pressure gases to
       drive the piston.
 5.  Inlet Manifold
     * A passage that supplies fresh air-fuel mixture (in petrol engines) or air
       (in diesel engines) from the carburetor/intake system to the cylinders.
 6.  Exhaust Manifold
     * Collects burnt gases from the cylinder through the exhaust valve.
     * Directs them to the exhaust pipe for release into the atmosphere.
 7.  Inlet & Exhaust Valves
     * Inlet valve: allows fresh air-fuel mixture (or air) into the cylinder.
     * Exhaust valve: expels burnt gases after combustion.
     * Timed precisely by the camshaft.
 8.  Spark Plug (only in petrol/ SI engines)
     * Provides the spark to ignite the compressed air-fuel mixture.
     * Initiates combustion at the right time.
 9.  Connecting Rod
     * Connects the piston to the crankshaft.
     * Transmits reciprocating motion of piston into rotary motion of
       crankshaft.
 10. Crankshaft
     * A rotating shaft that converts reciprocating piston motion into rotary
       motion.
     * Delivers power to the flywheel and vehicle transmission system.
 11. Piston Ring
     * Rings fitted on the outer circumference of the piston.
     * Functions:
       1. Sealing – prevents leakage of gases.
       2. Heat transfer – transfers heat from piston to cylinder wall.
       3. Lubrication control – regulates oil film on cylinder wall.
 12. Gudgeon Pin (Wrist Pin)
     * Connects the piston to the small end of the connecting rod.
     * Allows oscillating motion of connecting rod with respect to piston.
 13. Camshaft
     * A shaft with cams that operate the opening and closing of inlet and
       exhaust valves.
     * Driven by crankshaft (via timing belt/chain/gear).

Q2) FUNCTIONS OF LUBRICANT IN AUTOMOBILE ENGINES

Lubricants play a crucial role in the smooth and efficient working of IC
engines. Main functions are:
 1. Reduction of Friction – Forms an oil film between moving parts (like piston
    and cylinder), reducing friction and wear.
 2. Cooling – Carries away part of the heat generated due to friction and
    combustion.
 3. Sealing – Helps piston rings to form a seal between piston and cylinder,
    preventing gas leakage.
 4. Cleaning – Removes dirt, carbon deposits, and metal particles by suspending
    them in oil.
 5. Corrosion Protection – Forms a protective film on engine parts, preventing
    rust and chemical attack.
 6. Shock Absorption – Dampens the load and vibration on engine components.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


TYPES OF LUBRICANTS USED IN AUTOMOBILES

 1. Liquid Lubricants (Engine Oils)
    * Most widely used in IC engines.
    * Mineral oils (derived from petroleum) or synthetic oils.
    * Examples: SAE graded oils (SAE 20W-40, SAE 10W-30).
 2. Semi-Solid Lubricants (Grease)
    * Thick lubricant made by mixing oil with soap.
    * Used in wheel bearings, chassis, and other places where liquid oil is not
      suitable.
 3. Solid Lubricants
    * Examples: Graphite, Molybdenum disulphide (MoS₂), Teflon.
    * Used in high-temperature or extreme-pressure conditions where oils/greases
      fail.
 4. Synthetic Lubricants
    * Chemically prepared oils (like esters, silicones, polyglycol).
    * Provide superior thermal stability, oxidation resistance, and performance
      in extreme conditions.

Q3) 1. RADIATOR – FUNCTION

 * A heat exchanger used in the cooling system of an IC engine.
 * Transfers heat from hot coolant (coming from the engine) to the surrounding
   air.
 * Working principle: Hot coolant flows through thin tubes; fins attached to
   tubes increase surface area; air (from fan or vehicle motion) removes heat.
 * Maintains optimum operating temperature of the engine (~80–90 °C).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


2. THERMOSTAT – FUNCTION

 * A temperature-sensitive valve placed between the engine and radiator.
 * Function:
   * Keeps coolant circulating within the engine (bypassing the radiator) when
     engine is cold → helps quick warm-up.
   * Opens gradually as the coolant heats up (~80–85 °C), allowing coolant to
     flow to radiator for cooling.
 * Prevents overcooling and maintains efficient operating temperature.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


3. ANTI-FREEZE COMPOUNDS – FUNCTION

 * Added to the engine coolant to lower the freezing point and raise the boiling
   point of water.
 * Prevents coolant from freezing in cold climates and boiling in hot
   conditions.
 * Common anti-freeze agents: Ethylene glycol, Propylene glycol.
 * Mixed with water in different ratios (e.g., 50:50) to form coolant.
 * Also contains corrosion inhibitors to protect engine metals.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary (exam-ready):
 * Radiator: Removes heat from hot coolant.
 * Thermostat: Controls flow of coolant, maintains operating temperature.
 * Anti-freeze compounds: Prevent freezing/boiling of coolant, protect against
   corrosion.

Q4) DEFINITION

The cylinder arrangement (engine configuration) refers to how the cylinders are
physically placed in the engine block. This affects engine balance, size,
cooling, smoothness, and performance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


TYPES OF CYLINDER ARRANGEMENTS


1. V-ENGINE CYLINDER

 * Cylinders arranged in two banks forming a “V” shape.
 * Compact design with more cylinders in small space.
 * Smooth operation, commonly used in cars, trucks, and racing engines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


2. INLINE CYLINDER (STRAIGHT ENGINE)

 * All cylinders arranged in a single line (vertical or inclined).
 * Simple construction, easy maintenance.
 * Common in motorcycles, cars, and buses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


3. OPPOSED CYLINDER / BOXER ENGINE

 * Cylinders arranged horizontally, opposite to each other.
 * Provides good balance and a low center of gravity.
 * Used in sports cars (Subaru, Porsche) and aircraft.

Q5) DEFINITION OF CARBURETION

Carburetion is the process of mixing the appropriate amount of fuel with air in
an internal combustion engine (especially in spark-ignition/petrol engines) to
form a combustible mixture before it enters the cylinder.
 * Done by a device called the carburetor.
 * Objective: provide correct air–fuel ratio under different operating
   conditions (idling, acceleration, cruising, starting).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


FACTORS AFFECTING CARBURETION

 1. Engine Speed
    * At higher speeds, more air is drawn in → affects suction of fuel.
    * Carburetor must adjust fuel flow to maintain proper mixture.
 2. Temperature of Air
    * Warm air reduces density, requiring less fuel.
    * Cold air increases density, requiring more fuel.
 3. Fuel Characteristics
    * Volatility of fuel determines how easily it vaporizes.
    * High volatility fuels mix better with air.
 4. Air Velocity in Carburetor Venturi
    * Higher velocity at the venturi increases suction → draws more fuel.
 5. Load on Engine
    * Light load requires lean mixture (more air, less fuel).
    * Heavy load requires rich mixture (more fuel).
 6. Altitude / Atmospheric Pressure
    * At higher altitudes, air density decreases → mixture becomes rich unless
      adjusted.
 7. Design of Carburetor
    * Efficiency of mixing depends on venturi size, jet design, and atomization.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary for exam:
 * Carburetion = mixing of fuel and air in proper ratio before entering engine
   cylinder.
 * Factors: Engine speed, air temp, fuel volatility, venturi velocity, engine
   load, altitude, carburetor design.

Q6) DEFINITION OF EFI SYSTEM

Electronic Fuel Injection (EFI) is a system in which fuel is injected directly
into the intake manifold or combustion chamber using electronically controlled
injectors.
 * The amount and timing of fuel injection are controlled by an Electronic
   Control Unit (ECU).
 * Sensors (airflow, throttle position, oxygen, coolant temperature, etc.) send
   data to the ECU, which calculates the precise fuel requirement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


MERITS OF EFI SYSTEM

 1. Better fuel economy – accurate fuel supply reduces wastage.
 2. Improved performance – ensures optimum air-fuel mixture under all
    conditions.
 3. Lower emissions – better combustion reduces harmful gases.
 4. Easier cold starting – avoids need for choke, adjusts mixture automatically.
 5. Smoother engine operation – consistent fuel delivery, less knocking.
 6. Adaptability – adjusts fuel delivery depending on load, speed, altitude, and
    temperature.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


DEMERITS OF EFI SYSTEM

 1. High cost – more expensive than carburetor system.
 2. Complexity – requires sensors, ECU, injectors → complicated design.
 3. Maintenance difficulty – requires skilled labor and diagnostic tools.
 4. Sensitive to fuel quality – dirty fuel can clog injectors.
 5. Electrical dependency – system fails if ECU or sensors malfunction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary (exam-style):
 * EFI = electronically controlled system for precise fuel injection.
 * Merits: better economy, performance, emissions, smoothness.
 * Demerits: costly, complex, maintenance heavy, fuel-sensitive.

Q7) FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS OF INJECTION SYSTEM

In an IC engine (mainly diesel engines), the fuel injection system must:
 1. Meter the fuel – supply the correct quantity of fuel as per load/speed.
 2. Atomize the fuel – break liquid fuel into fine droplets for proper mixing.
 3. Distribute the fuel – deliver fuel evenly to all cylinders.
 4. Control injection timing – inject fuel at the correct moment of the cycle.
 5. Regulate injection pressure – ensure sufficient pressure to penetrate air in
    the cylinder.
 6. Shut off cleanly – no dribbling of fuel after injection.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


TYPES OF INJECTION SYSTEMS

A. ACCORDING TO METHOD OF INJECTION

 1. Air Injection System
    * Compressed air is used to inject fuel into the cylinder.
    * Heavy, bulky, outdated (used in early engines).
 2. Solid Injection System (Airless Injection)
    * Fuel is directly forced into the cylinder without compressed air.
    * Widely used in modern diesel engines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

B. ACCORDING TO ARRANGEMENT IN SOLID INJECTION

 1. Individual Pump System
    * Each cylinder has its own fuel pump and injector.
    * Accurate, but costly.
 2. Distributor System
    * Single pump sends fuel to different cylinders in sequence.
    * Compact, cheaper, used in small engines.
 3. Common Rail Injection System (CRDI)
    * Fuel stored at high pressure in a common rail → electronically controlled
      injectors deliver fuel to each cylinder.
    * Very precise, used in modern diesel cars/trucks.
 4. Unit Injector System
    * Each cylinder has a combined pump + injector.
    * Used in heavy-duty engines (trucks, marine engines).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary (exam-ready):
 * Injection system requirements: Meter, atomize, distribute, time, pressurize,
   shut-off.
 * Types:
   * Air injection (obsolete)
   * Solid injection: Individual pump, Distributor, Common Rail (CRDI), Unit
     injector

Q8) DEFINITION OF EXHAUST GAS RECIRCULATION (EGR)

Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) is an emission control technique in which a
portion of the exhaust gases is recirculated back into the engine intake
manifold, mixed with fresh air–fuel mixture, and then re-burnt in the combustion
chamber.
 * Purpose: To reduce Nitrogen Oxide (NOx) emissions by lowering peak combustion
   temperature.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


WORKING PRINCIPLE

 * A valve (EGR valve) controls the amount of exhaust gas recirculated.
 * When exhaust gases mix with incoming air, they dilute the oxygen
   concentration and absorb heat.
 * This reduces the maximum temperature inside the cylinder → thus less NOx
   formation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


ADVANTAGES OF EGR

 1. Reduces NOx emissions effectively.
 2. Improves engine efficiency at part load.
 3. Helps in meeting emission norms.


DISADVANTAGES OF EGR

 1. Increases soot and particulate formation.
 2. Can reduce engine performance if excessive.
 3. Adds complexity to engine design.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Exam-ready definition:
“Exhaust Gas Recirculation is a method of reducing NOx emission by recirculating
part of exhaust gases to the intake, thereby lowering peak combustion
temperature.”

Q9) DEFINITION OF SUPERCHARGING

Supercharging is the process of supplying air (or air–fuel mixture) to the
engine cylinder at a pressure higher than atmospheric pressure using a
compressor (called a supercharger) driven by the engine crankshaft.
 * Purpose: To increase the mass of air inducted → allows more fuel burning →
   higher power output from the same engine size.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


ADVANTAGES OF SUPERCHARGING

 1. Increased power output without increasing engine size.
 2. Improved volumetric efficiency (more air intake per cycle).
 3. Better power-to-weight ratio of the engine.
 4. Higher mechanical efficiency since engine friction losses remain nearly
    constant.
 5. Improved engine performance at high altitudes where air is thin.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


DISADVANTAGES OF SUPERCHARGING

 1. Increased mechanical stress on engine components due to higher pressure.
 2. Higher heat generation → requires efficient cooling.
 3. Reduces engine life if not designed properly.
 4. Consumes some engine power to drive the supercharger (mechanical load).
 5. Expensive and adds complexity to engine design.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary (exam-style):
 * Supercharging = supplying air to cylinder above atmospheric pressure using a
   compressor.
 * Advantages: More power, efficiency, better high-altitude performance.
 * Disadvantages: Stress, heating, reduced life, cost, power loss to drive
   compressor.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


Q10) DEFINITION OF TURBOCHARGING

Turbocharging is the process of forcing more air into the engine cylinder using
a turbine-driven compressor.
 * Unlike a supercharger (driven mechanically by the crankshaft), a turbocharger
   is powered by exhaust gases.
 * Thus, it uses otherwise wasted energy to improve engine power and efficiency.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


WORKING PRINCIPLE

 * Exhaust gases spin a turbine wheel.
 * Turbine drives a compressor wheel on the same shaft.
 * Compressor forces more air into the engine cylinder → allows more fuel
   combustion → more power.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


ADVANTAGES OF TURBOCHARGING

 1. Higher power output without increasing engine size.
 2. Better fuel efficiency since exhaust gas energy is utilized.
 3. Improved high-altitude performance (compensates for thin air).
 4. Compact and lighter than superchargers for the same boost.
 5. Reduces specific fuel consumption.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


DISADVANTAGES OF TURBOCHARGING

 1. Turbo lag – delay in response at low engine speeds.
 2. High operating temperatures – requires better lubrication and cooling.
 3. More complex than naturally aspirated engines.
 4. Costly maintenance – sensitive to oil quality, needs regular care.
 5. Reduced reliability if not properly maintained.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summary (exam-ready):
 * Turbocharging = boosting air pressure using an exhaust-driven
   turbine-compressor unit.
 * Advantages: More power, efficiency, good at high altitudes, compact.
 * Disadvantages: Turbo lag, heat, cost, complexity, maintenance.
